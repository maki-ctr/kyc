# K-05 Interview guide: opening a bank account as a newcomer in Belgium

*Personal project with a fictional bank (kycBank). Version 0.1, 26 Nov 2026.*

## Set-up

- **Who:** people who moved to Belgium in the last three years and opened, or tried to open, a Belgian account. Mix of EU and non-EU if possible.
- **How many:** three (two are enough for v0.2).
- **Length:** 20 minutes, video call or in person, English or Portuguese.
- **Recruit through:** Malick's pro bono association and network.
- **Never ask for:** account numbers, ID numbers, salary amounts, or copies of any document. If someone starts to share them, stop them politely.

## Consent (read aloud, then ask for a yes)

> Thank you for helping. I'm Malick, and I'm building a practice app for a made-up bank as a personal portfolio project. It isn't for any real bank. I'd like to hear how opening a bank account in Belgium went for you. There are no right answers, and you can skip any question or stop at any time. I'll take written notes without your name. I won't record audio or video unless you say yes now. I'll use what you tell me only in this project, and I'll quote you only without your name. Is that OK?

Note the answer: written notes only, or notes plus recording.

## Questions

1. When did you arrive in Belgium, and when did you first need a Belgian bank account? What for?
2. Walk me through how you opened it, from the moment you started looking. Which bank, online or in a branch?
3. Before you started, how did you find out which documents you needed? Was anything missing when you tried?
4. Was there a step where you got stuck or had to stop and come back later? What happened?
5. Did anyone ask you for itsme or a Belgian ID card? What did you do?
6. The bank probably asked what the account was for, where your money comes from, or about your job. How did those questions feel? Did anyone explain why they were asked?
7. How long did it take from starting to being able to receive a salary? What did that delay cost you, if anything (rent deposit, salary, bills)?
8. Did you have to go to a branch? Why, and how was it?
9. If you could change one thing about the process, what would it be?
10. Has your bank since asked you to update your details? How did that go?

Follow-up prompts: "Can you show me?", "What did you expect?", "What did you do next?". Don't suggest answers.

## Notes template (one file per person)

- Participant code: P1, P2 or P3 (no names)
- Date, length, language, recording: yes or no
- Arrival year; EU or non-EU; bank type; online or branch
- Timeline: start to first salary received
- Documents needed and where they found the list
- Stuck points, in the participant's words
- Reaction to purpose, source of funds and occupation questions
- itsme or eID barrier: yes or no, and what happened
- Branch visit: yes or no, and why
- One change they would make
- Refresh experience, if any
- Best quote (anonymous)

## Synthesis (one page, after the last interview)

1. What we heard: three to five findings, each with how many participants said it (for example "2 of 3").
2. Which K-01 assumptions held, which didn't, and which are still open.
3. What changes in flow 1 and flow 2 because of it, logged in the decision log.
